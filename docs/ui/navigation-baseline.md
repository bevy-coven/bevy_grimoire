# Getting around, as it is today

Generated by `crates/bevy_cauldron/tests/navigation.rs` (`navigation::`), which drives the editor from a
cold start on a fixture project by the keys and pointer actions a person uses. Rerun it with

```
cargo nextest run -p bevy_cauldron --test editor -E 'test(navigation::)'
```

**One press is one key event.** A chord is one press (`Cmd+O` counts once) and a typed character is
one. Pointer actions — a click, a wheel notch — are counted in their own column, because a route
that needs the wheel has no keyboard form at all.

**A row that does not arrive counts what the search cost.** For those the numbers are the work spent
establishing that there is no route — sixty wheel notches down the inspector, say — rather than the
price of a route, because there is no price to pay.

## What a later change is held to

- Any workspace in **1 press**.
- Any named thing in at most **Cmd+P, its name and Enter** — one press, some typing, one press.
- Back to where you were in **1 press**.

| Destination | Route | Keys | Pointer | Arrives |

| A named piece on the open map | click View, click Show outliner, wheel down to it, click its row | 0 | 3 | yes |

| A composition | press = (cross to the kit), press 3 | 2 | 0 | yes |

## What the numbers say

7 of 20 destinations cannot be reached at all. Every one that can be reached is reached either
by a chord the person has to know (`Cmd+O`, `Cmd+,`, the crossing key) or by the wheel: the
inspector is one long scroll, and no key moves it. That is why a workspace in one press, and a
name reached by typing it, are the targets.
