## Oracle: `contrast`, and why it cannot fail

For "text is hard to read" the command's oracle is `contrast`: every text's foreground and background meet the skin's minimum, or WCAG AA (4.5:1) where the skin sets none. It was measured twice and holds both times, by a wide margin.

- **On the capture's own pixels:** text `#E0DDD5` on card `#2A2722` is **10.96:1**.
- **In a throwaway whole-application test:** the What's new page at the capture's surface (1280x703, the real `LauncherPlugin`, `HomeTab::WhatsNew`), walking each sentence's `TextColor` to its nearest non-transparent `BackgroundColor`. It **passed on the base**, every sentence at **10.93:1** (fg srgba 0.88/0.865/0.835, bg 0.165/0.153/0.134).

A test that passes on the base proves nothing, so it was not committed, as the command requires.

## What a person has to decide

The complaint is the typeface: monospace prose reads "like a terminal". Making the page look better means choosing:

1. **Whether** Cauldron's prose surfaces get a proportional face: What's new, Learn, dialog bodies, and maybe the editor's descriptions. The chrome stays monospace, and so does the terminal.
2. **Which face, and its licence.** Nothing proportional ships today. It has to be MIT/Apache/OFL like FiraMono, which is OFL 1.1. Fira Sans is its natural pair.
3. **Where the line is drawn,** because `chrome::BODY_CHAR_W` and several callers (`badges`, `compose`) measure text by a fixed advance. Prose moved to a proportional face must leave those measurements on the monospace face, or the measurement needs text layout.

Once that is answered, the next pass's oracle is a `spec`: the What's new sentences are drawn in the chosen prose face, and the measure bound from `feedback-1793905698793` still holds.
